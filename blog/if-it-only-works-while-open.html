If it only works while open, it isn’t push

The OS will kill the process. The OEM will be worse. A notification that only works in the foreground is a toast with extra steps.

Copy changes by product — a prayer window, a trip status, an earning credit, a collab invite. The machinery does not. Register the device token. Refresh it. Drop it on uninstall and on a 404 from FCM or APNs. If you keep dead tokens, you are measuring vanity delivery, not reach.

Targeting is a product decision. A live stream should not fan out as N individual sends; that is a topic. Twelve seat updates should collapse into one “your bus moved.” Data messages sync unread and badge when the process is dead. Notification messages exist for the tray when the user is not in the app. Mixing those two because “it’s simpler” is how badges lie.

Quiet hours and retry have to respect platform backoff. A setTimeout loop is not a delivery policy. Deep links should open the ticket, the verse, the chat — not the home screen, which is how you train people to ignore you.

CEOs ask why retention dipped after a release. Often the answer is not the feature. It is a permission prompt, a rotated token, or a payload that woke the app and painted nothing. Push is infrastructure. Treat it that way, or it will fail quietly — which is the most expensive way to fail.
