A seat is a lock

Founders see a map of chairs. The product is whether two phones can buy the same row — and whether money and the ticket ever disagree.

Every booking screen is a lie that has to stay consistent. The user taps a seat. Another user taps it 400ms later on a worse network. A payment gateway retries. A webhook arrives twice. Someone kills the app on the confirm screen.

If your model is “the client reserved a chair,” you will oversell. The chair is not state. The lock is state. Hold it with a TTL long enough to pay, short enough that abandoned carts give the inventory back. Commit the ticket only after a confirmed charge. Treat the webhook as an idempotent event, never as “the user tapped pay.”

Refunds belong in the same story. Time-to-departure is a rule: 100% before 48 hours, then a step-down, then nothing. If refunds live in Slack, you do not have a payments product. You have an operations habit that will not scale past the first angry thread.

Gateways change — PayU hashes on the server, Razorpay, Stripe, mobile money. The invariant does not: double-tap never charges twice. A ticket never exists without money. The mobile UI is a citizen of that contract, not the source of truth.
