Case study

Shipping Legato in 9 months

Legato, a musician collaboration platform, needed iOS and Android apps live in 9 months for an investor demo: sign-in with Google and Apple, profiles with skills, discovery by name, skill, and proximity, and collaboration flows wired into chat and push notifications.

I own both sides — the Expo client and the API behind it — so mobile, backend, and dashboard never disagree.

The risk was the demo itself — one crash or one broken rollout in front of investors. I kept releases safe with Expo SDK upgrades, EAS staged rollouts, and feature flags, so unfinished collaboration paths never reached users before they proved out. Identity went through Better Auth on a custom Postgres adapter, with registration provisioning the profile, Stream Chat identity, and initial skills in one pass. Notifications went dual-channel, GetStream plus Firebase, with badge counts synced and taps deep-linking to the exact chat.

Result: live on both stores, zero crashes at demo. The same discipline runs in my other products: SRKAN works with zero signal, Jet Express sells offline across 30+ cities and syncs per tenant, Govihari totals on-device to match the server exactly, and KavooGo's earning loop is idempotent so a retry can never double-pay.

Search

Search is about turning messy, growing catalogs into something that feels instant. The critical problem is never the text box — it's ranking across different shapes of data without slowing down as volume grows or failing on weak networks.

Early work was about making discovery correct and fast. At SRKAN, I built multi-field search across programs, testimonials, and donations, each with different searchable attributes, with pagination and filters for status, featured content, and date ranges. For Jet Express, operating across 30+ cities, I built city, route, and station discovery with tenant-isolated queries, so onboarding a new location never interfered with existing operations.

Later the problem shifted to intent. At KavooGo, across English- and French-speaking African markets, I built campaign-matching around country, city, employment, education, salary, and eligibility, normalizing inconsistent location names so targeting stayed reliable. For Legato, I built hybrid discovery combining name search, skill matching, proximity with PostGIS, and collaboration preferences. The impact was the same in each case: the user finds the right thing in one try, which is what keeps them coming back.

Booking

Booking is the hardest system to get right, because a double-sell destroys trust instantly. The critical problem is always the same: two phones tap the same seat on a bad network, payment is slow to confirm, and the system has to hold inventory without ever getting stuck.

My rule: never complete a reservation before payment is verified. At Jet Express, across 30+ cities, I built the flow with Redis locks with expiration to hold the seat, idempotent events to survive retries, and async queues to carry confirmations without blocking the sale. For Govihari, where a boat has fixed capacity, I used reserve → pay → confirm, with failed or expired payments auto-cancelling and releasing the seat instead of leaving inventory stuck.

The same lifecycle protects money everywhere else. At KavooGo, I handled Stripe intents and checkout sessions with webhook and refund-status tracking so a retry resolves to the same transaction, never a new charge. For SRKAN, I applied it to donations on poor networks: verification, webhook-driven status, and receipts the donor can trust. For Legato, I gated rollouts with feature flags so new collaboration paths prove out before they scale. The impact is operational: no oversell at the gate, no stuck seats, no support tickets about money.

Payments

Payments are where engineering depth turns directly into business trust. The critical problems repeat: gateways lie, webhooks arrive twice or not at all, users retry on bad networks, and every edge case touches real money.

I hold one rule: verify first, handle everything idempotently. At SRKAN, I built PayU verification, webhook-driven donation status, multi-currency tracking, receipt uploads for manual verification, and automated emails for confirmations and SWIFT instructions. For Govihari, I integrated Razorpay order creation with server-side HMAC validation, created bookings inside database sessions tied to the payment lifecycle, and supported guest checkout without an account.

At scale the same discipline prevents revenue loss. At Jet Express, I built multi-gateway processing with company-specific selection, unique event records per company to stop duplicate webhook processing, and queued refunds with retries plus automatic seat release on expired or failed payments. For KavooGo, I fixed a Stripe contract mismatch while keeping the existing campaignId flow backward compatible, added a direct-amount flow, and extended webhooks to cover success, failure, checkout completion, and refunds. The impact: every payment resolves to one truth, and finance never chases ghosts.

Authentication

Authentication is never just login — it's how a dashboard learns to trust identity. The critical problems are isolation between tenants, roles that actually hold under real operations, and sessions that stay revoked once logged out.

I validate, isolate, then authorize on every request. At Jet Express, I built JWT authentication with company-level isolation — middleware that validates bearer tokens, checks revocation, loads the user, and attaches company context — plus role-based Socket.IO rooms so real-time traffic stays separated by company and responsibility. For SRKAN, I implemented hierarchical roles — super admin, admin, editor, user — through protected routes and reusable middleware.

Consumer identity brought different constraints. At Govihari, I integrated Firebase phone auth where the phone number is the primary identity, with persistent FCM token management tied to notification workflows. For Legato, I integrated Better Auth on a custom Sequelize adapter for PostgreSQL with Google and Apple sign-in and email OTP recovery, where registration also provisions the profile, Stream Chat identity, and initial skills. For KavooGo, I built separate guards for users, partners, admins, teams, and dashboards, each with its own access boundary and revocation checks. The impact is quiet but decisive: the right people see the right data, and nothing else.

Dashboards

Every app I ship needs the dashboard behind it — the screen where ops sees what the phones are doing. The critical problem is always complexity under pressure: bookings, fleet, money, and people changing at once, with different roles needing different truths from the same data.

I slice per domain with centralized auth, refresh, and real-time feeds. At Jet Express, I built a full BackOffice — bookings, trips, fleet, users, routes, stations, cities, tenants, subscriptions — with role-based routing for admin, manager, staff, and platform_admin, live trips and KPIs over Socket.IO, everything tenant-scoped by companyId.

The same pattern scales down to focused operations. At SRKAN, I built program stats, branch and date-range filtering, donation export to CSV/Excel, currency listing, and status tracking across pending, success, failed, and refunded. For Govihari, I built filtering by status, date, boat, and guide, cancelled bookings with refund tracking, boat-wise occupancy and revenue, today's view, and payment KPIs. For KavooGo, I built slices for users, campaigns, transactions, and notifications with RBAC-filtered navigation and user-to-campaign matching display. The impact: a founder sees the business in one screen, and staff act without asking engineering.

Notification System

Push is a delivery system, not a send call. The critical problems are brutal and consistent: the OS will kill the app, tokens rotate and die, iOS caps what you can schedule, and a message that arrives late might as well never have been sent.

I assume zero signal and a dead app, then build up. At SRKAN, built for villages with poor networks, I built local scheduled reminders with expo-notifications, rebuilding the plan from programs and schedules, capped at 60 items for iOS limits, 6 weeks ahead, 10-minute lead time, with Android channels, provisional iOS permissions, and multilingual titles. No server needed. For Jet Express, I built a full FCM pipeline with sendEachForMulticast, token arrays with invalid-token pruning and preference checks, queue fan-out to all customers or scoped to a trip or bus, per-user en/fr localization, DB persistence, and Socket.IO emit with routing data.

Other products needed different fallbacks for the same guarantee. At Govihari, I built token fetch and upsert on launch and after OTP, foreground alerts, background handlers, and backend send across stored tokens. For KavooGo, I built Expo Push with encrypted token storage, validation, ticket-status handling, DeviceNotRegistered cleanup, and Infobip SMS fallback when push can't deliver, with read/sent tracking. For Legato, I built dual push via GetStream and Firebase with email alongside, badge sync from unreadCount, and deep-links to the exact chat on tap. The impact: the message still arrives after the OS has killed the app — and that is what brings users back on day 30.
