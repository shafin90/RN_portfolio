Search

Search makes three promises, and only three. Nothing that should match is missing. The right things come first. It arrives before patience runs out. I learned to count on complaints, because every one I received reduced after the first month to a single sentence, and each sentence pointed at a different layer of the system. So I stopped writing code until I knew which sentence I was living in. Then I settle the shapes before the layers. What is each type searchable by, who owns each row, whether eligibility or intent has to be decided before anything is shown at all. After that the rules are firm and they travel well. Narrow at the query and never on the phone. Clean noisy names once at entry instead of forgiving them on every keystroke. Return ranked in a single trip with nothing merged on the device, and delete any second sort that quietly fights the true order.

What changes is the business behind it. A devotional library holds many shapes with different fields and has to answer instantly on weak networks while new items keep arriving, so each type gets its own fields and its own filters. A bus network holds the same shapes under many owners, where one company's rush must never touch another's numbers, so every list, query, and count stays scoped to its house. A rewards platform has to decide who may see something before it decides how to order it, so eligibility and remaining budget are checked first and noisy place names are normalized once at entry. A musician product has to decide intent before distance, because wrong order there means a wrong partner rather than an untidy list.

At SRKAN, programs, stories, and donations each carry their own searchable fields, with a dedicated transaction lookup beside the general search and export inheriting the same filters, so finance and the eye agree. At Jet Express, city, route, and station stay inside their own company, so adding a new location never disturbs the existing ones. At KavooGo, place names are cleaned once into one canonical list, then eligibility and available budget are checked before a campaign is ever shown. At Legato, name, skill, nearness, and intent are composed in one query, empty faces are filtered at the query level, and the phone's second sort is removed so the database order stands. The user judges in one try. One try that lands is retention, and one try that misses is deletion.

Booking

Booking is not a form. It is a promise about a seat, money, and time, and I learned that at a dock, watching what a double-sell costs in front of people. So I draw the states before I draw the screens: free, held, taken, cancelled, freed, each change naming its owner and its end. A hold with no end becomes stuck stock that nobody clears. A hold ending too fast breaks a genuine buyer while they are still typing their card number. Two buyers arriving on the last seat at the same second is a normal Friday, not an edge case. Hold duration, release ownership, and refund behavior are pricing and anger expressed as minutes, and they belong on paper with the founder's signature beside them, long before anyone opens an editor. Then I design the exits first, because that is where products quietly fail.

Every hold ends by itself, so nothing waits on a cleanup call. Ticket and money move together or not at all, so the two can never disagree. A repeated press finds its first result, so one intent gives one outcome. Guests stay findable by phone long after reinstall, because every guest is a future support ticket. Offline work waits its turn and rejoins with the server deciding clashes. Unfinished paths stay flagged until they prove out, and an empty response fails loudly instead of rendering silence.

What changes is what being wrong costs. A river boat leaves on time with fixed seats and a double-sell argues at the dock in front of passengers, so the rules lean to safety with short holds and instant self-return. A bus network closes each company's day separately with sellers working without signal, so the rules lean to separation, with per-company rejoin and one company's rush never starving another. Donations and rewards trade in trust, where a second charge deletes the app, so the rules lean to one outcome with proof the customer can hold.

At Govihari, holds live briefly and return quietly, guests book by phone and are found later by phone plus booking reference, and the bill counts on the device for an instant answer and again on the server so both sides trust one total. At Jet Express, offline sales persist and rejoin per company, abandoned checkouts sweep on expiry, seats release on failure, and a paid booking that cannot be completed refunds itself with retries that know their method. At SRKAN, a donation pressed three times on a dropping connection counts once and leaves one receipt. At KavooGo, a completed survey pays once from the right campaign and balance. At Legato, unfinished collaboration paths stay behind flags until they scale. No oversell at the gate, no stuck seats, no owner cleaning up after the app.

Payments

Payments are a promise the business makes to its customers, and the promise is simple: a retry can never charge twice. That belongs in the requirements, not in an engineer's head, because it is a business decision and it will eventually conflict with a nicer message. I learned this from a lying network. The reply dies after the charge, the screen says failed, the person presses again, and without design that is two charges. So I treat timeouts as routine traffic rather than exceptions, provider messages as arriving twice and out of order, and a closed browser as meaning nothing at all. I verify on the server and never trust the client callback.

From there the rules are firm. Every payment resolves to one truth with its kind, amount, and owner attached. Malformed or disagreeing orders are refused without throwing. Deposits confirm before balances move. Seats release on failure. Paid work that cannot be completed refunds by itself. Retries know their method and carry partial accounting, audit, and notice. Exhausted paths surface to ops instead of vanishing, and pending always means pending, never missing.

What changes is what the money means. A donation is trust the customer keeps, so it needs receipts, sometimes a human eye on a bank transfer, mailed instructions, and rates frozen at initiation. A boat ticket belongs to a guest with no account at all, so the bill has to match on both sides down to the rounding. A bus network settles per company across provider generations, so deposits confirm before balances move and one company's numbers never touch another's. A reward is budget, so it must exist before the campaign is shown, and the wallet has to show the stored truth rather than a mock.

At SRKAN, payments confirm provider-side with status following the provider through webhooks, receipts stored safely, confirmations and transfer instructions mailed, and rates frozen at payment start. At Govihari, the bill is recounted server-side and a mismatch rejects the order, with guests checking out by phone alone. At Jet Express, each company routes down one guarded path with type checks and limits, amounts that disagree are refused, a dismissed browser is not counted as failure, the status poll declares the outcome, and refunds retry by method with partial accounting and notice. At KavooGo, the wallet shows the real stored balance, negative amounts are guarded, limits apply at the edge, and contacts pre-register before money moves. The ledger has to close. No double charges on triple taps, no money without tickets, no tickets without money.

Authentication

Identity is infrastructure, not a login screen. Documents love login screens. They show the phone field, the code boxes, the cheerful success state, and then they stop, as if identity were a door you walk through once. I learned the difference from lockouts and orphans, because a login box without provisioning order, partial-failure behavior, and a revocation clock is a demo rather than a system. So I ask what identity even means for this product before choosing anything. Who is this person, what does each role get to touch, how does access die, and how does a guest come back later for a refund? Each of those answers is a promise about money and data, so they get settled on paper with the founder before any design begins.

Then everything the person needs is created in one pass, so nothing is orphaned and nothing races. Roles stay narrow, covering exactly what each one must and nothing more. Revocation actually revokes within a short window, rather than deleting a token from a device the thief is still holding. And every guest is treated as a future support ticket, so the guest's owner and upgrade path get defined now rather than argued about later.

What changes is the person on the other side. People who carry no email need a phone number that survives reinstalls and matches the dispatch records. People who arrive by social sign-in need profile, chat identity, tokens, and preferences created together, or not at all. Companies staffing drivers, cashiers, inspectors, managers, and platform admins need traffic and data separated by company and by responsibility. Rewards platforms split users, partners, admins, and teams, each with its own boundary and its own revocation.

At Jet Express, the code received at sign-up stays as the permanent code, so a returning traveler signs in without waiting for a message, while a forgotten code is reissued and replaces the old one, attempts count toward lockout, and tokens never ride back in responses. Roles split across drivers, cashiers, inspectors, managers, and platform admins, with per-company isolation underneath. At Legato, registration creates the user, profile, chat identity, and initial skills in one pass, startup waits for authentication and onboarding before it fetches, and login or logout never leaves navigation stuck. At Govihari, the phone is the identity and the device becomes known at the moment the code is verified. At KavooGo, separate guards keep users, partners, admins, teams, and dashboards apart. The result is quiet and decisive. The right people see the right data and nothing else, a stolen phone stops spending, and a dismissed worker stops working.

Dashboards

A dashboard is the founder's morning. They open one screen before anything else happens, and staff should be able to clear the day's work from it without calling engineering. If action requires a ticket, the dashboard failed no matter how it looks. So I build counts that close: verification queues narrowed by state, filters identical between a list and its export, totals per company, and audit wherever money moved. I learned this from mornings where exports disagreed with the lists they summarized, queues of paper tickets had no approver and no state, and one company's trips were visible to another because the query forgot who was asking.

The pattern is the same everywhere. I slice per domain with sign-in centralized and live feeds underneath, so each company sees only its own truth and one company's rush never touches another's numbers. Exports inherit the active filters rather than owning separate ones. Queues expose state so work gets cleared instead of exported as a mystery. Content and theme stay per company, so one house never wears another's storefront.

What changes is the morning itself. Many companies closing separate days need everything scoped per company, with live trips and per-company revenue and occupancy underneath. A giving office needs program stats with branch and date filters, export under those same filters, currency lists, and status tracked from pending through refunded. A dock needs today's boats, occupancy, and payment signals beside cancellations carrying their reason and return. A rewards operation needs users, campaigns, transactions, and notices side by side with stats and configurable rewards.

At Jet Express, the back office covers bookings, trips, fleet, crew, routes, stops, users, content, and theme per company, with verification queues narrowed by verification state, approval restricted to admins, typed ticket numbers accepted, and report filters able to exclude a category entirely. At SRKAN, program stats take branch and date filters while donation export inherits the active filters, with currencies listed and status tracked from pending to refunded. At Govihari, today's view sits beside boat-wise occupancy and revenue, cancelled bookings, and refund tracking. At KavooGo, users, campaigns, transactions, and notifications carry role-filtered navigation with matching displayed against campaigns. One screen at opening, action without tickets, and numbers that match the bank.

Notification System

The message is part of the feature, and silence is the most expensive failure there is. A crashed screen files a report. A failed payment files a ticket. An undelivered notice files nothing at all, so the user simply never comes back while the team debates the wording of messages that were never received. The copy was never the problem. The pipe was. I learned this the quiet way, which is the only way it teaches.

So I specify every notice like an agreement before design starts. Which events notify, through which channels in which order, with what fallback, opening exactly where, counted how, expiring when, and what proves it was delivered. Then I build up from the worst case, assuming no signal and a dead app, because that is the only assumption that never gets falsified.

What changes is the job the message has to do. A prayer reminder keeps a habit, so it must fire with no server at all. A departure warning prevents a missed bus, so it must arrive localized and on time, because a warning delivered an hour late is misinformation. A payout notice keeps trust, so it must carry its proof. A follow notice restarts a loop. Marketing never earns a fallback and bookings always do, and the price of that fallback is a budget line the founder signs before launch rather than discovers on the first invoice.

At SRKAN, prayer reminders rebuild from programs and daily schedules on every launch, in each program's own language, with quiet permission first, so they fire with no server at all. At Jet Express, tokens register on launch and again at verification, trip notices travel localized over personal channels with foreground alerts and background persistence, and queued bursts survive rush hour. At Govihari, tokens are fetched and upserted on launch and after verification, alerts present in the foreground and persist in the background. At KavooGo, push falls back to SMS when it cannot deliver, with per-message read and sent tracking. At Legato, following pings the followed person, badges reconcile from the true unread count, and a tap opens that exact thread. The message arrives after the system has killed the app, and that is what brings people back on day thirty.
