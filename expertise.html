Search

I learned search from complaints. Every one reduced to a single sentence: something that should match is missing, the order is wrong, or it arrives too late. I now refuse to write code before knowing which sentence I am living in, because each points at a different layer and each demands a different fix.

My habit is shapes first. What each type is searchable by, who owns each row, whether eligibility, order, or intent must be decided first. I narrow at query and never on the phone. I clean noisy names once at entry instead of forgiving them on every keystroke. I return ranked in one trip with nothing merged on the device, and I delete any second sort that fights the true order.

What I keep: the user judges in one try. One try that lands is retention. One try that misses is deletion.

Booking

I learned booking at a dock, watching what a double-sell costs in front of people. Since then I draw states with the founder before screens: free, held, taken, cancelled, freed, each with its owner and its clock. Hold duration, release ownership, refund behavior — these are pricing and anger expressed as minutes, and they belong on paper with a signature.

My habit is exits first. Every hold ends by itself. Ticket and money move together or not at all. A repeated press finds its first result. Guests stay findable by phone long after reinstall. Offline work waits its turn and rejoins with the server deciding clashes. Unfinished paths stay flagged until proven, and emptiness fails loudly instead of rendering silence.

What I keep: no oversell at the gate, no stuck seats, no owner cleaning up after the app.

Payments

Payments

I learned payments from a lying network. The reply dies after the charge, the screen says failed, the person presses again — and without design that is two charges. I now treat timeouts as routine traffic, provider messages as arriving twice and out of order, and a closed browser as nothing at all. I verify on the server and never trust the client callback.

My habit is one truth per payment with its kind, amount, and owner attached. Malformed or disagreeing orders are refused without throwing. Deposits confirm before balances move. Seats release on failure. Paid-but-incomplete work refunds by itself with retries that know their method. Exhausted paths surface to ops instead of vanishing. Pending always means pending, never missing.

What I keep: a ledger that closes. No double charges on triple taps, no money without tickets, no tickets without money.

Authentication

Authentication

I learned identity from lockouts and orphans. A login box without provisioning order, partial-failure behavior, and a revocation clock is a demo, not a system. I now ask what identity even means for this product first: what survives reinstalls, what each role may touch, how access dies, how a guest returns later. Each answer is a promise about money and data, so it belongs on paper with the founder.

My habit is fit and narrow. Where people carry no email, the phone survives and codes stay permanent for returners with fresh ones replacing on forget. Where people arrive by OAuth, one path provisions user, profile, chat identity, and preferences together with nothing orphaned and nothing racing. Roles gate exactly what they must, revocation actually revokes, and each house sees only its truth.

What I keep: the right people see the right data and nothing else. A stolen phone stops spending. A dismissed worker stops working.

Dashboards

Dashboards

I learned dashboards from mornings. Founders open one screen before anything else, and staff should clear the day's work from it without calling engineering. If action needs a ticket, the dashboard failed no matter how it looks. I now build counts that close: verification queues by state, filters identical between lists and their exports, per-house totals with audit where money moved.

My habit is slice per domain with sign-in central and live feeds underneath. Each house sees only its truth. Rush in one never touches another's numbers. Content and theme stay per house. The morning clears from one screen — approve, refund, dispatch — and numbers match the bank.

What I keep: a readable business. One screen at opening, action without tickets.

Notification System

Notification System

I learned notices from silence. A crashed screen files a report. A failed payment files a ticket. An undelivered notice files nothing — the user simply never returns while the team rewrites copy for messages never received. The copy was never the problem. The pipe was. I now specify every notice like an agreement before design starts: which events, through which channels in which order, opening where, counted how, expiring when, and what proves delivery.

My habit is job first. Habits must fire with no server at all. Warnings must arrive localized and on time — late is misinformation. Trust notices must carry their proof. Loops restart on a ping. Bookings always earn a fallback and marketing never does, with the fallback's price signed as budget before launch, not discovered after the first invoice. Every notice opens its exact screen with back intact and names only what changed. Informed once, never spammed thrice.

What I keep: the message arrives after the system kills the app. That is what brings people back on day thirty.
