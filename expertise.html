Search

My philosophy: finding is a promise with three clauses. Nothing that should match is missing. The right things come first. It arrives before patience runs out. When complaints come, they reduce to one of those sentences, and each points at a different layer. I diagnose which sentence I am living in before writing code, because each demands a different fix.

The approach varies because the business varies. A devotional library holds many shapes with different fields, and the need is instant on weak networks while new items keep arriving. A bus network holds identical shapes but many owners, and adding a city must never disturb old ones. A rewards platform decides eligibility before order, with noisy place names and two languages. A musician search decides intent before distance, where wrong order means a wrong partner.

Across my career I kept the same discipline with different weights. Devotional finding stays per type with narrowing at query and a dedicated transaction lookup beside general search, export under identical filters. Buses stay inside their own house on every list, query, and count. Rewards clean place names once at entry to one canonical list, then check eligibility and budget before showing. Musicians order by presence then nearness with intent as a hard gate, one query ranked, the phone's second sort removed so the database order stands, nothing merged on the device.

Founders buy this because the user judges in one try. One try that lands is retention. One try that misses is deletion.

Booking

My philosophy: inventory has states, every hold has an end, ticket and money move together, and every exit is owned on paper before code. Free, held, taken, cancelled, freed — each change names its owner and its clock. A hold with no end becomes stuck stock. A hold ending too fast breaks a genuine buyer mid-payment. Two buyers on the last seat is a normal Friday, not an edge case.

The approach varies because the cost of being wrong varies. A river boat leaves on time with fixed seats, and a double-sell argues at the dock — so the rule leans to safety with short holds and instant self-return. A bus network closes each house's day separately with offline sellers — so the rule leans to separation with per-house rejoin and the server deciding clashes. Donations and rewards trade in trust — so the rule leans to one outcome with receipts and single payouts.

Across my career: boats hold briefly and return quietly, guests book by phone with phone-plus-reference enough to return after reinstall, the bill counts on the device for instant answers and again on the server for one trusted total. Buses persist sales offline and rejoin per house, sweep the abandoned on expiry, release seats on failure, and refund paid-but-incomplete bookings with method-aware retries. Donations pressed three times count once. Surveys pay once from the right campaign and balance. Unfinished collaboration paths stay flagged until proven.

Founders buy this because mistakes here are not tickets — they are turned-away passengers, lost money, and support mornings. Mine have neither oversell at the gate nor stuck seats.

Payments

My philosophy: verify on the server, never trust the client callback, and let a repeated press find its first result. Money moves on flaky networks where replies die after the charge — the screen says failed, the person presses again, and without design that is two charges. Every payment resolves to one truth with its kind, amount, and owner attached, or finance chases ghosts for weeks.

The approach varies because what the money means varies. A donation is trust with receipts a donor keeps, sometimes needing a human eye on a bank transfer and mailed instructions across currencies with rates frozen at initiation. A boat ticket is a guest with no account whose bill must match on both sides. A bus network settles per house across provider generations. A reward is budget that must exist before showing and a wallet that must show the stored truth, never a mock.

Across my career: devotional payments confirm provider-side with webhook-driven status, receipt uploads set aside safely, confirmations mailed. Boat orders recount the bill server-side and refuse mismatch. Buses route each house down the same guarded path with type checks and limits, confirm deposits by webhook before balances move, keep the payer as reported, and let the status poll — not a closed browser — declare the outcome. Rewards move in clean steps with pre-registered contacts and method-aware retries carrying partial accounting, audit, and notice.

Founders buy this because the ledger closes. No double charges on triple taps, no money without tickets, no tickets without money, no pending that silently means missing.

Authentication

My philosophy: identity fits the user, roles stay narrow, revocation actually revokes, and registration leaves nothing orphaned. Who the person is, what each role may touch, how access dies, and how a guest returns later — decided on paper with the founder, because each answer is a promise about money and data, not a screen.

The approach varies because the person varies. Rural travelers carry no email, so the phone number is the identity that survives reinstalls and matches dispatch records. Musicians arrive by OAuth, so sign-in flows through one path with recovery reusing the same session. Bus networks staff drivers, cashiers, inspectors, managers, and platform admins, so traffic and data separate by house and responsibility. Rewards split users, partners, admins, and teams with distinct boundaries.

Across my career: saved codes stay permanent for returners with fresh codes replacing on forget, attempts counting toward lockout, tokens never riding back in responses, numbers normalized to world format. OAuth provisions user, profile, chat identity, and preferences together — no orphans, no races — with startup waiting for auth and onboarding before fetching. Narrow roles gate publishing, editing, refunding, approving, and reading, with per-house isolation underneath and token checks that actually reject.

Founders buy this because the quiet guarantee is the business: the right people see the right data and nothing else, a stolen phone stops spending, a dismissed driver stops driving, and support restores a guest by phone without engineering.

Dashboards

My philosophy: each house sees only its truth and can act on it without calling engineering. Counts that do not close are decorations. Verification queues, report filters, and per-house totals exist so the morning's work clears from one screen — approve, refund, dispatch, restock — with audit where money moved.

The approach varies because the morning varies. A bus network opens with many houses closing separate days: bookings, trips, fleet, crew, routes, stops, users, content, theme — live trips, per-house revenue and occupancy, verification queues, inspector views. A devotional office opens with giving: program stats, branch and date filters, export under active filters, currency lists, status from pending to refunded. A dock opens with today's boats, occupancy, and payment signals beside cancels with reasons and returns.

Across my career I sliced per domain with centralized sign-in and live feeds underneath: full back offices with role-based routing, focused giving and ticketing views with identical filters to their exports, campaign operations with users, transactions, and notices side by side with stats and configurable rewards. One company's rush never touches another's numbers because isolation runs through query, count, cache, queue, and room.

Founders buy this because the business becomes readable. One screen at opening, action without tickets, numbers that match the bank.

Notification System

My philosophy: the message is part of the feature, and silence is the most expensive failure. A crashed screen files a report. A failed payment files a ticket. An undelivered notice files nothing — the user simply never returns while the team rewrites copy for messages never received. I specify every notice like an agreement: which events, through which channels in which order, opening where, counted how, expiring when, and what proves delivery.

The approach varies because the notice's job varies. A prayer reminder keeps a habit and must fire with no server at all. A departure warning prevents a missed bus and must arrive localized and on time. A payout notice keeps trust and must carry amount with campaign attached. A follow ping restarts a loop. Marketing never earns a fallback; bookings always do — and the fallback's price is a budget line the founder signs before launch, not after the first invoice.

Across my career: devotional reminders rebuild from programs and schedules on every launch in each program's language with quiet permission first. Trip notices travel localized over personal channels with push config deployed alongside, tokens registered on launch and again at verification, foreground alerts and background persistence, queued bursts surviving rush hour. Every notice opens its exact booking, thread, payout, or match with back intact and names only what changed. Where push cannot reach, the message still arrives by the next channel — informed once, never spammed thrice.

Founders buy this because retention curves are built from these clauses. The message arrives after the system kills the app — and that is what brings people back on day thirty.
