Search

How I think: finding is a promise with three clauses, and I refuse to write code before knowing which clause is broken. Nothing that should match is missing. The right things come first. It arrives before patience runs out. Every search complaint I have ever received reduced, after the first month, to one of those three sentences. Missing points at matching and normalization. Wrong order points at ranking and query shape. Slow points at indexes and payload. Teams that skip the diagnosis rebuild the text box. I fix the layer.

How I approach: shapes first, then layers. I list what each type is searchable by, who owns each row, and whether the business needs eligibility, order, or intent decided first. A devotional library holds many shapes with different fields and must answer instantly on weak networks while new items keep arriving. A bus network holds identical shapes under many owners where a new city must never disturb old ones. A rewards platform must decide who may see before how to order, across noisy place names and two languages. A musician search must decide intent before distance, because wrong order there means a wrong partner, not an ugly list.

What I faced: devotional searches where each type needed different fields and filters while lists kept growing underneath browsing. Bus discovery where one house's rush could leak into another's numbers. Reward targeting where a city typed three ways defeated equality, in two tongues, with budget behind every impression. Musician discovery where null faces ranked first and a second sort on the phone quietly re-sorted rounded strings and broke the true order.

How I survive: rules that travel with me now. Narrow at query, never on the phone. Clean noisy names once at entry to one canonical list instead of fuzzy-matching every keystroke. Return ranked in one round trip with no merging on the device and no extra queries per row. Keep the database order standing by deleting the phone's second sort. Scope every list, query, and count to its house. Keep a dedicated transaction lookup beside general search, with export under identical filters so finance and the eye agree.

Founders buy this because the user judges in one try. One try that lands is retention. One try that misses is deletion.

Booking

How I think: inventory has states, every hold has an end, ticket and money move together, and every exit is owned on paper before code. Free, held, taken, cancelled, freed — each change names its owner and its clock. A hold with no end becomes stuck stock. A hold ending too fast breaks a genuine buyer mid-payment. Two buyers on the last seat is a normal Friday, not an edge case. I draw the states with the founder first, because hold duration, release ownership, and refund behavior are pricing and anger expressed as minutes.

How I approach: I learn what being wrong costs in that business, then lean the rules that way. A river boat leaves on time with fixed seats and a double-sell argues at the dock in front of people, so the rule leans to safety. A bus network closes each house's day separately with sellers working without signal, so the rule leans to separation. Donations and rewards trade in trust where a second charge deletes the app, so the rule leans to one outcome with proof.

What I faced: boats where abandoned checkouts locked seats for days and guests carried no email to return by. Buses where sellers sold offline and two sales met on one seat, where hosts in other zones hid today's trips, where stale replays arrived as empty lists that rendered silence. Donations pressed three times on dropping connections. Rewards where a retry meant a second payout. Collaboration paths unfinished before a demo that could not crash.

How I survive: holds live briefly and return quietly with no cleanup call. Guests book by phone with phone-plus-reference enough to return after reinstall. The bill counts on the device for instant answers and again on the server for one trusted total. Offline sales persist and rejoin per house with the server deciding clashes and the phone re-rendering quietly. The abandoned sweep on expiry, seats release on failure, paid-but-incomplete bookings refund with method-aware retries carrying partial accounting and notice. Unfinished paths stay flagged until proven. Emptiness fails loudly instead of rendering silence.

Founders buy this because mistakes here are not tickets. They are turned-away passengers, lost money, and support mornings. Mine have neither oversell at the gate nor stuck seats.

Payments

How I think: verify on the server, never trust the client callback, and let a repeated press find its first result. Money moves on flaky networks where replies die after the charge. The screen says failed, the person presses again, and without design that is two charges. Timeouts lie as routine traffic, provider messages arrive twice and out of order, and a closed browser is not a failed payment. Every payment resolves to one truth with its kind, amount, and owner attached, or finance chases ghosts for weeks.

How I approach: I follow what the money means in that business. A donation is trust with receipts a donor keeps, sometimes needing a human eye on a bank transfer and mailed instructions across currencies with rates frozen at initiation. A boat ticket is a guest with no account whose bill must match on both sides to the rounding. A bus network settles per house across provider generations with deposits confirmed before balances move. A reward is budget that must exist before showing and a wallet that must show the stored truth, never a mock.

What I faced: donations where the client claimed success the provider never saw, and multi-currency drift between initiation and settlement. Boat bills that drifted on rounding between device and server. Buses where malformed references threw instead of refusing, where amounts disagreed silently, where a dismissed browser was counted as failure, where refunds went down while the provider slept. Rewards where negative balances and mock fallbacks lied to the eye.

How I survive: one guarded path for every provider generation with type checks and request limits at the edge. Confirmation provider-side, status following the provider through webhooks, deposits confirmed before balances move, the payer kept as reported. Orders that disagree or arrive malformed are refused without throwing. Fulfillment releases the seat on failure. Paid-but-incomplete bookings refund by themselves with retries that know their method, carrying partial accounting, audit, and notice. Exhausted paths surface to ops instead of vanishing. Pending always means pending, never missing.

Founders buy this because the ledger closes. No double charges on triple taps, no money without tickets, no tickets without money.

Authentication

How I think: identity fits the user, roles stay narrow, revocation actually revokes, and registration leaves nothing orphaned. Who the person is, what each role may touch, how access dies, and how a guest returns later — I decide these on paper with the founder first, because each answer is a promise about money and data, not a screen. A login box without provisioning order, partial-failure behavior, and a revocation clock is a demo, not a system.

How I approach: I ask what identity even means for this product. Rural travelers carry no email, so the phone number must survive reinstalls and match dispatch records. Musicians arrive by OAuth with profiles, chat identities, tokens, and tastes to provision at once — so I define the atomicity: if step four of six fails, is the person registered or not. Bus networks staff drivers, cashiers, inspectors, managers, and platform admins — so traffic and data separate by house and responsibility. Every guest is a future support ticket, so I define the guest's owner and upgrade path now.

What I faced: device-vendor auth that had to come home to server messages without breaking a traveler. Stuck navigation after login and logout that left people stranded between screens. Chat connecting twice on restart with stale sockets. Token checks that waved through the revoked. Company traffic bleeding across houses. Guests unfindable after reinstall with refunds owed and no account to attach them to.

How I survive: saved codes stay permanent for returners with fresh codes replacing on forget, attempts counting toward lockout, tokens never riding back in responses, numbers normalized to world format with sending limits. OAuth provisions user, profile, chat identity, and preferences together with startup waiting for auth and onboarding before fetching, observers keeping navigation true, disconnect-before-connect keeping sockets clean. Narrow roles gate publishing, editing, refunding, approving, and reading with per-house isolation underneath and rejection that actually rejects within the minute. Support restores a guest by phone plus reference without engineering.

Founders buy this because the quiet guarantee is the business: the right people see the right data and nothing else, a stolen phone stops spending, a dismissed driver stops driving.

Dashboards

How I think: each house sees only its truth and can act on it without calling engineering. Counts that do not close are decorations. Verification queues, report filters, and per-house totals exist so the morning's work clears from one screen — approve, refund, dispatch — with audit everywhere money moved. If staff must ask engineering to act, the dashboard failed no matter how it looks.

How I approach: I open with the founder's morning. A bus network opens with many houses closing separate days, so everything slices per house with live trips underneath and isolation through query, count, cache, queue, and room — one house's rush never touches another's numbers. A devotional office opens with giving, so stats, branch and date filters, and export share identical filters with the lists they summarize. A dock opens with today's boats, occupancy, and payment signals beside cancels carrying reasons and returns.

What I faced: dashboards showing company A's trips to company B. Exports disagreeing with their lists because filters differed. Queues of paper tickets with no approver and no state. Inspectors with no view of their own. Campaign operations split across screens with rewards configured nowhere visible. Counts that matched nothing at closing.

How I survive: I slice per domain with centralized sign-in and live feeds underneath — full back offices with role-based routing, focused giving and ticketing views whose exports inherit the active filters, currency lists beside status from pending to refunded, verification queues narrowed by state with admin-only approval, report filters that set aside categories cleanly, inspector views with their own tab, campaign operations with users, transactions, and notices side by side with stats and configurable rewards. Content and theme stay per house so one storefront never wears another's dress.

Founders buy this because the business becomes readable. One screen at opening, action without tickets, numbers that match the bank.

Notification System

How I think: the message is part of the feature, and silence is the most expensive failure. A crashed screen files a report. A failed payment files a ticket. An undelivered notice files nothing — the user simply never returns while the team rewrites copy for messages never received. I specify every notice like an agreement before design starts: which events, through which channels in which order, opening where, counted how, expiring when, quiet hours, and what proves delivery.

How I approach: the notice's job decides its channels. A prayer reminder keeps a habit and must fire with no server at all. A departure warning prevents a missed bus and must arrive localized and on time — late is misinformation. A payout notice keeps trust and must carry amount with campaign attached. A follow ping restarts a loop. Marketing never earns a fallback; bookings always do. And the fallback's price is a budget line the founder signs before launch, not after the first invoice — channel priority is money disguised as technology.

What I faced: reminders capped by the system that needed six weeks of plans pruned to the nearest with lead time for the OS to wake. Tokens dying silently, permissions denied, manufacturers killing background work. Notices opening the app root instead of their booking. Dead tokens endlessly retried. Critical failures with no escalation while the team debated wording. Badges lying about unread counts.

How I survive: devotional reminders rebuild from programs and schedules on every launch in each program's language with quiet permission first — firing with no server. Trip notices travel localized over personal channels with push config deployed alongside, tokens registered on launch and again at verification, foreground alerts and background persistence, queued bursts surviving rush hour. Every notice opens its exact booking, thread, payout, or match with back intact and names only what changed. Dead tokens prune with logs, tickets track per message, undeliverable criticals escalate to the next channel, badges reconcile from the true count on every foreground. Informed once, never spammed thrice.

Founders buy this because retention curves are built from these clauses. The message arrives after the system kills the app — and that is what brings people back on day thirty.
