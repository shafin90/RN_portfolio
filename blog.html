Notes on systems that have to be correct.

Everything here came out of a production failure I had to diagnose, or a rule I had to write down before the damage. No frameworks, no tool tours. Just the parts of mobile work that break silently.

  • Payments · 6 min

    A retry must find the first result

    Any request your app sends may be executed more than once, and nobody will know. Why a second charge ends customer trust, why the promise belongs to the business rather than the engineer, and the three places idempotency stops being simple.
  • Booking · 6 min

    A hold must end by itself

    A hold with no expiry is not a booking, it is inventory loss. Why releasing a seat is a pricing decision with an owner's name on it, the three mechanisms that recover it, and why the expiry rate is really a measure of your checkout.
  • Search · 6 min

    Clean it once on the way in

    Forgiving a misspelled city on every keystroke is cheaper to build and more expensive to keep. Why normalising names against a canonical list on the way in is the only approach that later lets you count, join, and report by place.
  • Notifications · 6 min

    After the app is dead

    A message that only works while the app is open is not a notification. The dead-app case is the majority case in production, and the fallback that rescues a failed delivery is a budget line the owner has to sign before launch.
  • Thesis · 6 min

    The feature is not the product. The business is.

    Search, booking, payment, identity, and messages are not features. They are decisions, and the right shape of each is set by what being wrong costs in your specific business. Why reusing last quarter's solution is how features ship broken.
  • Analysis · 9 min

    Requirements are a system, not a document

    Every failed mobile project I have been called into had a requirements document. It was reviewed, signed, and described every screen in careful detail. Then money went missing, seats sold twice, and notifications never arrived. The screens were correct. The system was not.
