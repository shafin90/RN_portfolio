Case Study

Legato — Musician collaboration platform, MVP to production

Legato is where I learned to hold a product calm.

A musician profile, discovery, follow, block, groups, chat, push. Small US team, both stores. My part was the phone as it is — killed app, denied permission, null photo, stale socket, unloaded list — and the server agreeing with it.

I start behind the screen. A wrong order means a wrong partner. A follow with no notice kills the loop. A tap that lands at root kills the reply. A crash in a demo room kills trust. So I work failure first.

Search, I kept honest. Fuzzy name, skill overlap on cleaned tags, nearness by true geography, open-to as hard gate. Faces first, then distance. One query returns ranked. I removed the second sort on the phone — it re-sorted rounded strings and broke the database order. I filtered empty photos at query. No merging on the phone, no extra trips. When you type fast, old requests cancel. When place is denied, browsing stays. When lists load late, they fall back to empty with guards. New arrivals never duplicate your browsing.

Matching, I shipped to Explore. Scores run apart from search so the two never fight. Base with capped skill bonus, intent through casual/serious/professional fit, nearness close/far, activity recent/silent by true last-active time. Teach finds learn, looking-for maps to open-to across jam, band, collaboration, teacher, student. Limited per person with expiry. Precomputed daily and on profile change, cleaned weekly, reads cheap. I put it behind a flag with viewed, dismissed, and refresh paths and funnel, and I fixed the time it scores on — without true activity every score reads active and wrong.

Identity, I kept single. Registration creates user, profile, chat identity, and skills together. No orphans, no race. Google and Apple through the same path, recovery by code. I fixed login and logout sticking navigation, and made startup wait for auth and onboarding before fetching nearby.

Follow, I kept notified without risking the follow — token and email after commit, failure never rolls back the relation. Block, I kept everywhere including search within followers. Chat, I made reconnect clean by disconnecting first and resetting flags, gated delete by capability, kept names straight. Open-to to chat, inbox from profile with back to profile, custom-message entry. My rule for taps: that exact thread, back intact.

State, I moved writes into actions — direct writes threw and took screens. I unified three lists into one with stable dependencies — reload loops and remounts ended, infinite scroll wired. Media, I tracked compression from the camera and settled timestamped uploads with thumbnails — no stretched faces, no stuck states.

Release, I kept shippable while adding. Major SDK and React upgrades with camera, picker, auth, and push intact. Crash reporting with user context from the start, then I burned the queue — thread seen, social activity, connection, null handlers. Tutorial and tour, birthday, location control, loading states.

What stayed with me: unfinished paths behind flags, end-to-end on two phones per release, typed contracts so mobile and API cannot drift. The product held — profile to discovery to follow to chat to push, ordered, scored, notified, back intact — because every failure had an owner before it reached a musician.

Case Study

SRKAN — Devotional platform, steady on weak networks

SRKAN is where I learned to serve people the network forgot.

Villages with intermittent signal, small phones, users who need prayer, programs, and giving to simply work. My part was reading that never needs signal, reminders that fire without a server, and donations a donor can trust on a bad radio.

I start from the morning. Someone opens the app before dawn. Home must show today instantly from what is already there, then refresh quietly without losing scroll. Reading scrolls smooth with steady rows that only redraw when language changes. Audio holds one lock — new playback releases the old — with lock-screen controls that survive calls and Bluetooth drops. Position returns after the system kills the app. Images stay light with cached thumbnails so small phones hold their frame rate.

Reminders, I kept local. The plan rebuilds from programs and daily schedules on every launch, in the language of each program, with quiet permission first so value proves before the prompt. No signal needed. The reminder fires in airplane mode because the schedule lives on the phone.

Finding, I kept per type. Programs by title and place, stories by name and healing, donations by name and transaction — each narrowed by status, featured, branch, currency, and date range at query time. Donations keep a dedicated transaction lookup beside the general search, plus export with the same filters and a currency list for finance. Admin lists that stay small filter where they stand. What the eye sees is what the query asked for, nothing merged on the phone.

Giving, I kept careful. Payment confirmed on the server, never trusted from the client. Status follows the provider through webhooks. Receipts upload for bank transfers that need a human eye, stored away safely. Confirmations and transfer instructions go out by email. A repeated press on a dropping connection finds its first result — one intent, one charge, one receipt. Money in more than one currency freezes its rate at the start so finance never chases drift.

Daily rhythm, I kept truthful to place. Schedules carry sessions with dates and zones, branches carry live and recorded video that fall back to empty shapes instead of breaking the screen. Roles stay narrow — who may publish, edit, or read — with tokens checked and revoked cleanly. Uploads arrive compressed within limits. Contact writes to mail with validation. Tests guard the endpoints that move money and mail.

What stayed with me: design for no coverage, small memory, and a dead process — and the product works everywhere. The donor who presses three times, the reader with no bars, the reminder at dawn — none of them should ever need to think about the network.

Case Study

Govihari — River-boat ticketing, fixed seats, calm boarding

Govihari is where I learned what a fixed seat really means.

Boats between forest landings with set capacity and set times. If one seat goes to two people, the problem shows up at the dock, in front of people. Many travelers carry no email — the phone number is the identity. My part was booking that frees itself, money that never parts from its ticket, and an app that stays light enough for the field.

I start from boarding. Only boats that are active and still have seats are shown. A press holds a seat for a short life. If money does not arrive, the seat comes back by itself. No stuck list, no cleanup call. The ticket is written only with confirmed payment, never before. A repeated press on a weak network finds its first result — one intent, one charge.

Guests stay guests. No account needed to book. The phone is verified by code, the device becomes known at that moment, and phone plus booking reference is enough to find the trip later — even after reinstall. The bill is counted on the phone so toggling meals, vehicles, or add-ons answers instantly with taxes rounded up, and counted again on the server so both sides trust one total.

Cancelling follows the clock. Every cancel carries its reason, and the return follows a living policy that changes with nearness to departure. A cancelled ticket rests under past bookings, never beside active ones. Once cancelled it never looks confirmed again, and finance sees pending where payout waits.

The ticket must reach the hand. Tickets render to a file that opens, shares, and downloads again without a second wait — including where newer Android storage moved the ground. Passenger counts and ages are validated at entry so a boat never sails overfull on paper. Each screen keeps its own state instead of one central knot, and every screen was trimmed until the frame budget held.

Messages close the loop. Tokens register on launch and again after verification, alerts present in foreground and persist in background, and the dashboard can reach one device or many through a queue that survives bursts. Guards at the edge — request limits, strict cross-origin rules, containerized runs tested under concurrent load — keep the dock calm on busy mornings.

What stayed with me: a seat with an end, money with its ticket, a guest findable by phone, a cancel with its reason and return. The owner never cleans up after the app.

Case Study

KavooGo — Rewards that pay once, in two languages

KavooGo is where I learned money must be boring.

People watch and answer to earn, then withdraw to mobile money. English and French side by side. If a survey pays twice or never, trust leaves the same day. My part was eligibility that reads true, payouts that move once, referrals that land, and screens that speak both languages.

I start from who may see what. Country, city, work, schooling, pay band, family, gender, account — person and campaign carry the same fields. Names arrive noisy, so I clean at saving and at reading with one canonical place list and shared maps for work, schooling, and pay. A city typed three ways still resolves to one. What the eye sees in profile is the name, what the check uses underneath is the same truth.

Earning follows verify, then record, then reward. Watching counts only past its share, completion is caught by address intercept with fallback navigation and a calm manual submit that never fires early. The server checks against campaign rules, records once, and credits the wallet in the same guarded step with the balance held safe against negatives and repeats. A retry finds its first result. Budget is checked before showing — what is left must cover the reward — and the wallet shown is the real stored balance, never a mock.

Providers change, the rule does not. Card, mobile money, top-ups and payouts move through the same guarded path with type checks and request limits, deposits confirmed by webhook before the balance moves. Amounts stay in clean steps, contacts pre-register before money moves, and every money webhook carries its kind with amount and campaign attached so finance reads one story.

Referrals must land once. Links open the right screen even cold, survive navigation conflicts, and attach after login with the code and rewards present in the response. Self-invite refuses. Reward arrives with its own notice. Stats and configurable rewards stay visible to ops.

Identity left the device vendor and came home to server SMS — codes never ride back in responses. Notices turned contextual, naming only what changed, each opening its exact screen. Every hard string across dozens of screens moved into translation with both languages completed and deduped, so the product never flickers tongues.

What stayed with me: clean once at entry, check eligibility and budget before showing, pay in one guarded step, land every link, speak both languages fully. The earner who retries on a weak network gets one payout and one message.

Case Study

JetExpress — Inter-city buses, many companies, one truth each

JetExpress is where I learned a bus company closes its own day.

Many companies, many cities, sellers with weak signal, two phones on one seat. Each company counts its own money, seats, and trips. My part was five surfaces — backend, passenger app, staff app, dashboard, website — that agree on seat, money, time, and who may touch what.

I start from the timetable. Trips lead from routes, crews ride with fleets rather than typed names, and a driver's day derives from rotation — so a driver change never orphans a trip. Stops can switch off individually with fares snapshotted at booking and an alight gate that refuses what was never sold. When crew is missing, all gaps report at once instead of the first only. Assignment mistakes surface at creation, not at departure.

Days stay honest to the world clock. Day filters pin to the shared day so a host in another zone never hides today's trips. Cities resolve past accents and case and return every candidate instead of guessing one. Responses refuse caching where a stale replay once arrived as an empty list — the app now fails loudly on emptiness instead of rendering silence.

Identity fits returners. The code received at sign-up stays as the permanent code, so a known traveler signs in by typing it with no message, and a forgotten one receives a fresh code by message that replaces it. Live codes and saved hashes both verify, attempts count toward lockout, tokens never ride back in responses, numbers normalize to world format with sending limits and quiet periods. The vendor path moved from device auth to server messages without breaking a single traveler.

Money and seat move together or not at all. Orders refuse amounts that disagree and malformed references without throwing. Fulfillment releases the seat on failure. Abandoned checkouts sweep on expiry. A paid booking that cannot complete refunds by itself, with retries that know their method, partial accounting, audit, and notice — and the payer's number kept as reported. The status poll stays authoritative, so closing the browser is never counted as failure. Each provider generation replaced the last down the same guarded path.

Paper tickets get a human path. A typed number opens a verification request, approval stays admin-only, and reports narrow by that verification state or set aside a category entirely. Content and theme stay per company, so one house never dresses another's storefront.

Booking survives the field. Passenger sales, details, and profile persist offline and rejoin in turn per company, with the server deciding clashes and the phone re-rendering quietly. Inspectors scan faster with their own verification tab and no prefilled secrets. Timeouts, render crashes, and silent token failures are handled instead of swallowed. Trip notices arrive localized over personal sockets, and dashboard mail moved to a proper sender with the push config traveling with deploys.

What stayed with me: route first, fleet with crew, day by world clock, code that remembers returners, money with its seat, paper with its approver, offline that rejoins per house. Every company sees only its truth — and trusts it at closing.
